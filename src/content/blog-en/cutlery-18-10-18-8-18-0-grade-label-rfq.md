“18/10 cutlery” is a familiar sales description, but it is not a complete purchase specification for every knife, fork and spoon in a proposed set. The two numbers are shorthand associated with chromium and nickel content; they do not identify which piece in this particular order uses which steel, nor do they provide a batch result. If a quote uses **18/10, 18/8 or 18/0**, ask the supplier to translate the wording into an agreed component-level grade and supporting record before treating it as an order requirement.

The [British Stainless Steel Association's cutlery-grade note](https://bssa.org.uk/bssa_articles/cutlery-stainless-steel-grades/) explains the common 18/x descriptions and cautions that “18/10” does not by itself imply a higher actual nickel content than the 18/8 designation. Yumingxing's [cutlery collection](https://www.mteng.ltd/en/stainless-steel-cutlery/) likewise asks buyers to agree the exact grade of knife, fork and spoon separately, and warns against automatic equivalence between 18/x language and a formal grade. Neither source certifies the material of an unconfirmed Yumingxing order.

## Turn a marketing description into an RFQ row

| Field | Buyer should write | Supplier reply to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Component | Dinner knife, fork, spoon and any additional piece separately | Which item the statement covers |
| Sales wording | The exact 18/10, 18/8 or 18/0 phrase being quoted | Whether it is a description or a binding requirement |
| Agreed grade | Formal grade/designation for each relevant component | Written component specification, not a set-wide assumption |
| Construction | Whether the knife blade and handle share one specification | Identified construction and material by part where relevant |
| Sample and batch | Approved sample revision and delivered lot reference | A link between sample, order and shipment record |
| Verification | Document or qualified analysis needed for the buyer's purpose | Scope, issuer, sample identity and date |
| Claim release | Text proposed for listing, box or customer reply | Approval only after the exact claim is supported |

The table intentionally does not assign 18/10, 18/8 or 18/0 to a Yumingxing SKU. It also avoids assuming that a knife blade has the same material as a fork or its own handle. First establish what the supplier actually offers for the selected model and configuration. If grade verification is a contractual condition, define the document and testing method that will apply to the identified components and batch.

Do not use appearance or a quick magnetic check to “prove” the grade: magnetic response is only a sorting observation. Nor does a grade name by itself establish food-contact suitability for the destination or performance under a particular cleaning routine; these require separate, product-specific evidence.

Before releasing retail or catalogue copy, compare every material phrase with the signed component specification. If the supplier can only confirm a broad sales description, leave a precise grade claim out of the pack until the evidence is available. That makes the RFQ useful to manufacturing, inspection and marketing without inventing a material guarantee.
