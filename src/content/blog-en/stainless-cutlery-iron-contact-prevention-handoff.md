A polished stainless-steel cutlery sample can leave a workshop clean and still pick up foreign iron particles during handling or storage. For a buyer who has seen rust-coloured marks, the useful next procurement question is preventive: **where could the proposed knife, fork or spoon touch ordinary steel or grinding debris, and what controls are used?** Do not infer a factory's actual route from a generic photograph.

The [British Stainless Steel Association](https://bssa.org.uk/bssa_articles/iron-contamination-and-rust-staining-on-stainless-steel/) identifies contact with non-stainless equipment, shared tools and carbon-steel grinding debris as possible contamination sources. It advises avoiding contamination during storage, handling and fabrication. Yumingxing's [factory page](https://www.mteng.ltd/en/factory/) asks buyers to confirm which stages of the chosen model are in-house or externally supplied. These are grounds for questions, not proof that a specific supplier has a problem or uses any particular equipment.

## Walk the chosen model through contact points

| Stage | Buyer asks | Evidence or reply to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Material receipt | How are stainless components identified and separated? | Lot and component reference |
| Forming | What surfaces and tools touch the parts? | Model-specific process description |
| Finishing | How is ordinary-steel debris kept away from polished pieces? | Segregation and cleaning method |
| Transfer | What trays, racks or containers carry unfinished pieces? | Contact-material and handling explanation |
| Interim storage | Are pieces exposed to mixed-metal work or moisture? | Storage arrangement and responsible team |
| Packing | What touches each visible surface before sealing? | Final handling and inspection check |
| Follow-up | Who investigates a mark and preserves a sample? | Escalation owner and record link |

Ask the supplier to mark which stages apply to the exact configuration and which are externally supplied. The buyer need not prescribe a proprietary process; a written route and control owner make a review possible. If visible marks later appear, retain affected and comparison pieces and agree a qualified investigation. A brown mark alone does not identify its cause or the base-metal grade, and this worksheet is not a chemical cleaning instruction.

Add the agreed contact controls to the model-specific quality brief. Revisit them when the finishing route, equipment, handling container or subcontractor changes.
