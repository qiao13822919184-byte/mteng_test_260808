A cutlery sample can look suitable in a catalogue but feel different during table setting, guest use or the restaurant's cleaning routine. Before a bulk order, a hospitality buyer can run a **small, controlled service trial** on the exact knife, fork and spoon being considered. This scorecard captures buyer observations; it is not a laboratory test, product guarantee or proof of dishwasher suitability.

Yumingxing's [hotel and restaurant page](/en/hotel-restaurant-cutlery/) positions cutlery selection around service needs, while its [buying process](/en/) calls for specification and sample confirmation before bulk approval. Independent cutlery testing organization [CATRA lists product evaluation](https://www.catra.org/testing-services/testing-product-evaluation/), including dishwasher resistance. A restaurant's informal wash observation is not a substitute for a defined test or evidence supporting a published care claim.

## Fix the sample and trial conditions first

| Trial field | Record before and after use |
|---|---|
| Sample identity | Model, component name, material/finish stated by supplier and sample revision. |
| Service scenario | The actual place setting or meal type in which each piece will be used. |
| Handling observation | Comfort, balance, grip and obvious edge or tine concerns, recorded piece by piece. |
| Appearance | How the pieces look together under the venue's normal table lighting and alongside existing stock. |
| Cleaning route | The venue's actual method and conditions; record what was done, not a blanket “machine safe” conclusion. |
| Decision and owner | Accept, seek a revised sample or reject; list the observed reason and approver. |

Use the same service scenario when comparing two samples. Keep the tested sample or its identifier and dated notes, including photographs if useful. Ask staff to describe what happened rather than assigning an invented performance rating. A concern such as “handle feels awkward during this service task” is a trial observation; it does not establish a material defect or a universal failure.

The [restaurant assortment worksheet](/en/blog/restaurant-cutlery-service-assortment-worksheet/) defines which pieces are needed, and the [sample approval guide](/en/blog/cutlery-sample-approval-before-bulk-production/) helps turn a chosen trial sample into a controlled order reference. If the venue needs a documented cleaning-performance claim or destination-specific proof, state the exact product and conditions and request a suitable product-specific evaluation. Send the trial record with the [enquiry](/en/contact/) before approving the bulk configuration.
