A photo of a marked spoon may start a complaint, but it may not give a technical reviewer enough context to distinguish manufacturing, handling, cleaning or later contact effects. The buyer's first task is to preserve the **questioned physical piece and its history** without deciding the cause from appearance alone. If an item could present a safety concern, remove it from service under the site's normal safety procedure before further handling.

CATRA describes [cutlery defect assessment and metallurgical failure investigation](https://www.catra.org/testing-services/testing-product-evaluation/). Those services show why a defined sample and question matter; they do not diagnose an unexamined piece. Our [service-issue record](/en/blog/cutlery-service-issue-buyer-record/) covers the overall incident. This card focuses on the physical sample handed to the supplier or independent evaluator.

## Build a sample packet, not a conclusion

| Item | Buyer records | Why it travels with the sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Questioned piece | Component type, model, finish, visible condition and unique temporary ID | Keeps the finding tied to one object |
| Comparison piece | Unused or unaffected item from the same order, with its own ID | Gives the reviewer a relevant reference without assuming identical history |
| Lot link | Order, pack or carton reference and any available component batch | Identifies the population that may need review |
| Use history | When first used, cleaning method, chemistry if known, storage and observed change | Separates recorded conditions from guesses |
| Photos | Overall views plus close-ups before further cleaning or disassembly | Preserves the as-received state |
| Transfers | Who held, packed and received each item, with dates and condition notes | Reduces confusion between samples |

Ask the evaluator how to pack and whether cleaning or destructive testing should wait. Do not polish away a mark, combine different lots in one unlabeled bag, or call an unaffected comparison piece proof that the questioned one was misused. If a component is missing, say so rather than inventing a substitute.

The final handoff should state the buyer's question: for example, “What is this surface change, and what additional evidence would distinguish possible causes?” Keep customer observations, supplier response and independent findings in separate fields. A sample record enables investigation; it is not a safety certification, root-cause finding or blanket judgment on the rest of the shipment.
