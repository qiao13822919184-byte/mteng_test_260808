A required delivery date is not yet a usable cutlery order schedule. A private-label buyer may still need to settle the exact pieces, artwork, sample, packaging and inspection brief. Put those decisions into a milestone sheet **before committing the date to a retail launch or hospitality opening**. The sheet below is a planning tool, not a supplier lead-time quote or delivery guarantee.

Yumingxing's [buying process](/en/) asks buyers to share a target date, confirm specification and sample, agree production checks, and then confirm packing and shipment. Its [RFQ guide](/en/blog/what-to-include-in-a-cutlery-rfq/) asks for sample timing, production lead time and shipment arrangements separately. Those are distinct milestones; none should silently stand in for arrival at the destination.

## Work backward, then request a forward confirmation

| Milestone | Buyer must provide or approve | Supplier must confirm | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Required availability | Destination and date goods must be usable, plus any receiving buffer | Whether the requested timing merits a feasibility review | Dated sourcing brief |
| Specification freeze | Model, piece-by-piece material, finish, logo and set composition | Feasible configuration and unresolved questions | Controlled specification revision |
| Sample and artwork approval | Decision owner and approval criteria | Sample route and artwork proof route | Approved sample ID and artwork version |
| Order release | Quantity by SKU, packaging, carton marks and agreed terms | Accepted order scope and production timing | Purchase order and acknowledgement |
| Production and check | Any agreed inspection scope and documents | Check point and route for exceptions | Report or documented exception decision |
| Shipment handoff | Shipping details and destination contact | Packing and dispatch information under agreed terms | Packing list and shipment reference |

Start with the date when stock must be usable and ask which steps must finish before that date. Record the person responsible and a *confirmed* date for every handoff; leave the date blank when it has not been confirmed. A target date written in the first enquiry is an input to feasibility, not an approved commitment. If sample or artwork approval slips, request an updated schedule rather than preserving the old date by assumption.

## Keep approval dependencies visible

For a standard catalogue model, some work may be simpler than for a new tool or structural change, but the route must still be confirmed for that model. The [existing-model, private-label and tooling decision sheet](/en/blog/cutlery-customization-route-existing-model-private-label-new-tooling/) helps identify the route. The [sample approval record](/en/blog/cutlery-sample-approval-before-bulk-production/) helps stop a production date being based on an unapproved reference.

Before issuing a purchase order, mark each milestone **confirmed**, **awaiting decision**, or **not applicable**, with an owner. Send the completed brief through the [enquiry page](/en/contact/) and ask for separate confirmation of sample timing, production timing and the shipping handoff. Do not infer transit, customs or arrival dates from a production estimate.
