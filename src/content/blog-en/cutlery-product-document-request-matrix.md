“Please send your certificates” is too broad for a mixed cutlery order. A document for one steel sample, an earlier product revision or a different destination may not answer the buyer's actual question. Instead, send a **document request matrix** tied to the proposed order. This is a communication tool, not a declaration that any particular document exists or that a product complies with a law.

Yumingxing's [quality and documents guidance](/en/quality-compliance/) tells buyers to request information relevant to the actual product and destination. Its [RFQ guide](/en/blog/what-to-include-in-a-cutlery-rfq/) asks for component-level material and the target market. These two inputs should accompany any document request. A generic logo or scanned cover page alone cannot establish that a record applies to the knife, fork and spoon in the proposed set.

## Describe the question before naming the document

| Matrix column | What the buyer should enter |
|---|---|
| Order identity | Proposed model, SKU, drawing or specification revision, and the particular knife, fork or spoon. |
| Destination and intended use | Market and use that the buyer needs assessed; ask a qualified local reviewer which requirements matter. |
| Question to answer | Material identity, a stated performance claim, or another product-specific question; keep these separate. |
| Record requested | Ask the supplier what declaration, material record or independent report is available for that question. |
| Match check | Confirm the record names or can be linked to the relevant component, material, sample or batch and current revision. |
| Gap and decision | Note missing scope or outdated versions, the person resolving the gap, and whether a new evaluation is needed. |

Keep **available**, **requested**, and **verified for this order** as different statuses. A record may be authentic yet still cover a different material or design. If a logo, coating or handle construction changes, ask whether the existing evidence remains applicable instead of assuming it does. Retain the full document, its issue date, the linked product identifier and the supplier's explanation of scope.

The [sample approval record](/en/blog/cutlery-sample-approval-before-bulk-production/) helps identify the accepted product revision. Use the [RFQ checklist](/en/blog/what-to-include-in-a-cutlery-rfq/) to confirm the order inputs. Send the matrix through the [enquiry page](/en/contact/) with the proposed component list. Ask the local compliance specialist to decide the destination's applicable rules; this article does not name a universal certificate or legal standard.
