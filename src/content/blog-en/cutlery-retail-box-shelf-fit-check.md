A boxed stainless-steel cutlery set may fit a shipping carton yet fail a buyer's shelf plan if it is taller than the usable opening, hides the intended front panel when laid flat, or leaves no room for staff to lift it out. Before approving a retail pack, compare **the final packed unit in its intended display orientation** with the buyer's actual shelf space. This is a different question from whether loose utensils fit a storage tray.

Yumingxing's [cutlery RFQ guide](/en/blog/what-to-include-in-a-cutlery-rfq/) asks for piece count, packaging reference, dimensions where important, and carton marks. The [GS1 Package and Product Measurement Standard](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/ppm/) describes repeatable package measurements and warns that nominal dimensions do not necessarily match the way a pack is presented on a shelf. Use those facts to ask for both the packaged dimensions and the actual display arrangement; do not infer shelf fit from a catalogue photograph.

## Put the pack and shelf in one check sheet

| Field | Buyer should record | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Retail unit identity | Cutlery set and component count, packaging version, closed-box reference | Avoid measuring the wrong pack |
| Closed-pack dimensions | External width, height and depth in a stated unit and measurement orientation | Establish the supplied pack envelope |
| Display orientation | Upright, flat or another agreed position; which panel faces the customer | Translate dimensions into a shelf footprint |
| Usable shelf space | Clear width, height and depth after rails, dividers or shelf lips | Compare against real available space |
| Handling allowance | Clearance for replenishment, removal and any opening feature | Prevent a mathematically fitting but awkward display |
| Facing plan | Desired number of packs across and behind, with a separate plan for each variant | Avoid mixing shelf capacity with carton quantity |
| Evidence and decision | Pack mock-up, shelf photo or drawing revision, checker, date and unresolved points | Keep approval tied to the tested version |

For a simple row of packs, **usable shelf width divided by the displayed facing width** gives only an upper bound on how many fronts might fit. Reserve the buyer's required clearance first, then round down to a whole pack. This does not prove that packs can be removed, that the display is stable, or that the next shelf above leaves enough height. A physical mock-up of the intended quantity should decide those questions.

## Keep retail pack and transit case separate

Ask the supplier for the dimensions of the **closed retail unit** and, if relevant, any shelf-ready display case as separate items. A shipping carton dimension cannot stand in for the consumer pack. If the display case is opened or converted in store, record its dimensions in the display state too. Agree which panel is front and whether the pack must stand without support; request a mock-up if those details determine placement.

Approve the shelf arrangement only against an identified pack revision and buyer shelf drawing. If the number of cutlery pieces, insert, window, sleeve or box structure changes, remeasure and repeat the fit check. This worksheet does not claim a particular Yumingxing pack size, retailer requirement or approved display capacity.
