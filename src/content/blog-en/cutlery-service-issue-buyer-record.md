When a hotel or restaurant reports a mark, loose handle, changed finish or another concern after cutlery enters service, a quick photograph rarely establishes the cause. The buying team first needs to identify **which component and batch are being discussed**, what was observed, and what happened before the report. A clear record supports a useful supplier conversation without calling an observation a confirmed product defect.

Yumingxing's [quality and documents guidance](/en/quality-compliance/) asks buyers to match inspections and reports to the exact product, component and material batch. CATRA lists [cutlery evaluation methods](https://www.catra.org/testing-services/testing-product-evaluation/) for attributes such as corrosion and dishwasher resistance. The existence of those methods does not show that a particular item has been tested or that an observed issue has any specific cause.

## Use one row per distinct observation

| Record field | What the buyer should capture | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Product identity | Supplier reference, component, finish, set configuration and any available lot or carton mark | Avoid mixing different pieces and revisions |
| Report context | Site, date first noticed, reporter and where the piece was used | Separate the observation from later retellings |
| Observation | What can be seen or felt, its location on the item, and whether it appeared on more than one piece | Describe evidence without naming a cause |
| Exposure history | Known cleaning, storage and handling steps, with unknowns marked as unknown | Define what further questions are needed |
| Quantity basis | Number inspected, number showing the same observation and the population they came from | Keep an isolated case distinct from a broader pattern |
| Evidence | Overall and close-up photos, retained reference item and approved specification or sample version | Allow like-for-like comparison |
| Immediate handling | Which pieces were set aside, who holds them and whether use continues pending review | Prevent evidence from being lost |
| Next decision | Supplier question, additional evidence needed, owner and response date | Move the case forward without pre-judging it |

Do not turn the “quantity basis” row into a defect rate unless the inspected population and selection method are clear. A single photograph may show appearance but not composition, test history, cleaning conditions or whether two pieces came from the same production group. Keep the original item and images identifiable; do not alter or polish the area before the agreed review if that would erase evidence.

## Agree what would resolve the question

Compare the reported piece with the approved specification and an unaffected reference of the same component, where available. Ask the supplier which product-specific records exist and what additional inspection or independent evaluation would be appropriate. If the issue concerns a performance property, agree the test question, sample identity and responsible party before treating any report as applicable. CATRA's list of possible tests is a route to discuss, not a result for this order.

Record the outcome as **observation confirmed**, **more evidence needed**, or **cause and disposition agreed in writing**. Keep any commercial remedy, responsibility or deadline tied to the actual contract and the parties' written decision. This service-issue sheet is for an item already in use; a [receiving discrepancy record](/en/blog/cutlery-arrival-receiving-discrepancy-record/) addresses what was noticed when cartons first arrived.
