## 1. Build a narrow range around replenishment, not a headline growth rate

Our procurement hypothesis for New Zealand is a compact core range, a separately tested gift set, and online replacement pieces that can travel with a larger basket. The evidence supports testing this structure; it does not establish national bestsellers, demand per store or an optimal opening quantity.

Stats NZ's June 2026 quarter snapshot reports seasonally adjusted retail sales value of about NZD 33 billion, up 0.9% from the previous quarter, while sales volume fell 0.5%. These are broad retail measures, including businesses outside homeware. More dollars spent therefore cannot be read as more cutlery units sold. [Stats NZ, published 24 September 2026](https://www.stats.govt.nz/news/economic-snapshot-households-and-businesses-experience-higher-fuel-prices-in-the-june-2026-quarter/)

NZ Post reports 2025 online spending above NZD 12.7 billion, up 10%, with transactions up 6% and average basket value up 4%. Its revised methodology incorporates more payment channels through Dot Loves Data and recalculates comparative periods. Do not splice this figure onto an older, narrower card-only series and announce that e-commerce doubled. Its Q3 2026 update reports NZD 3.14 billion online spending, up 10.7% year on year, with domestic retailers taking 79%. Neither series isolates flatware. [NZ Post annual analysis](https://www.nzpostbusinessiq.co.nz/latest-ecommerce-insights/growth-shifts-favouring-local-retailers-2025) · [Q3 update, 29 October 2026](https://www.nzpostbusinessiq.co.nz/latest-ecommerce-insights/online-retail-builds-momentum-ahead-peak)

A timely demand signal is promotional competition: Retail NZ's 11 November release says the main sales season is under way and some Black Friday promotions have already started. This industry observation supports separating promotional and regular-price sell-through in a buying trial. It is not evidence that cutlery demand itself has accelerated. [Retail NZ](https://retail.kiwi/insights-media/media-releases/key-retail-promotional-season-singles-day/)

## 4. For supermarkets: constrain variants before committing depth

The 16-piece entry products and several 24-piece sets justify a starting comparison between a four-person core and a six-person upgrade. Keep handle shape and finish stable within a family so that a shopper can understand the upgrade. A 56-piece gift offer should have its own demand test and explicit seven-type contents rather than share a replenishment forecast with the core 24-piece set.

Ask competing suppliers to quote the same bill of materials: utensil counts, individual weights, length, thickness, alloy evidence by component, finish, care claim and packaging. A stainless knife may need a different alloy specification from a fork; an 18/10 claim for a set is not evidence that every component has identical chemistry. The sample-price ladder is a positioning reference only. It cannot yield a target FOB price without taxes, handling, distribution, returns and the buyer's margin assumptions.

For the shelf trial, request a retail pack and a filled outer carton. Measure usable facing width and shelf depth rather than copy a competitor's product name. IKEA's14×4×23 cm FÖRNUFT pack and25×5×34 cm MARTORP pack show why extra utensil types can also change presentation space. Orientation, hang-tab strength, barcode readability and protection of fork tips should be checked on the actual proposed pack.

Choose pilot stores across the locations you actually serve and set review dates before allocating chainwide volume. Track units per store per week, regular versus promotional sales, returns for missing pieces, and weeks of stock by set size. Negotiate split releases or a smaller replenishment carton only after the supplier confirms feasibility and pricing. No public sample here establishes an MOQ, lead time or a specific number of cartons that every New Zealand store should hold.

## 5. For e-commerce: replacement pieces need basket economics

IKEA's own delivery policy supplies a concrete constraint. Parcel delivery requires an order of at least NZD 20 after discounts. For parcels up to 8 kg, published non-member fees are 12 for the North Island and 14 for the South Island; IKEA Family fees are 7 and 9 respectively. Its parcel service has a 14 kg maximum and size/volume limits. Actual eligibility and charges depend on the address and order. [IKEA New Zealand delivery](https://www.ikea.com/nz/en/customer-service/services/delivery/)

The 3.75 four-fork pack and 5.50 MOPSIG set therefore do not individually meet that minimum. A 39.99 IKEA 365+ 24-piece set plus the published non-member parcel fee illustrates consumer totals of 51.99 in the North Island and 53.99 in the South Island, before any checkout-specific change. These are basket examples, not your courier contract, commercial fulfilment cost or guaranteed checkout prices.

For a seller, this points to a testable long-tail strategy: offer compatible fork/spoon replenishment as an add-on, collection item or combined shipment. Keep replacement patterns identifiable by measurements and clear photographs. Do not promise compatibility simply because both products are called stainless steel. Start with the replacements for your own best-supported core range, then expand only if order frequency and contribution justify the extra stock locations.

Measure contribution on a consistent tax basis after product cost, actual pick/pack and carrier charges, platform/payment fees, packaging, refunds and missing-piece replacements. Split the results by island and service zone. A parcel-level check should include movement inside the box, polished surfaces rubbing, knife tips, barcode scanning and item counting. Public retail package weights are useful planning inputs; the final protected shipping parcel still needs to be measured.

## 6. Shared Australia–New Zealand packaging needs separate legal decisions

Shared structural packaging is worth testing where contents, dimensions and supplier production are genuinely identical. Several IKEA article numbers and pack dimensions also appear in the Australian range reviewed for this series. That is evidence of a possible physical packaging approach, not permission to copy a competitor's artwork or assume Australian legal wording applies in New Zealand.

New Zealand's October 2026 tariff lists ordinary rates of 5% for 8215.20.00 and 8215.99.01, while the relevant CN preferential column is Free subject to origin rules. Customs' consolidated concessions separately includes 981606C for described stainless steel tableware under 82.15. These are different routes: a broker must confirm the actual classification and concession scope or origin evidence. Free customs duty does not make all import taxes and charges disappear. [Working tariff](https://www.customs.govt.nz/media/qafctvly/section-xv-effective-20-october-2026.pdf) · [Concession list, printed page 134](https://www.customs.govt.nz/media/uf4fpo42/consolidated-list-of-approvals.pdf) · [Customs guidance on claiming China preference](https://www.customs.govt.nz/information-releases-and-legislation/legislation/international-agreements/free-trade-agreements/new-zealand-china-free-trade-agreement)

For consumer supply, the Consumer Guarantees Act's sections 6–7 address acceptable quality, including safety and durability, and section 5A addresses delivery commitments. The Fair Trading Act section 12A requires a reasonable basis for substantiable representations; section 13 prohibits false or misleading claims including composition, grade, quantity and origin. Apply these controls to both the carton and online listing, including alloy and dishwasher claims. [Consumer Guarantees Act](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/91/en/latest/) · [Fair Trading Act 12A](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/121/en/latest/sections/DLM6156605/) · [Fair Trading Act 13](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/121/en/latest/sections/DLM96908/)

The government's current consumer-information-standards list is product-specific; it does not establish a universal stainless-flatware origin-label rule. Confirm the requirements for the actual goods and packaging, and retain truthful origin information rather than claiming a blanket labelling exemption. [Consumer Protection](https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/consumer-laws/consumer-information-standards)

Food-contact requirements also need a New Zealand scope check. FSANZ states that Chapters 3 and 4 apply only in Australia. New Zealand's Food Regulations 2015 include food-business provisions on preventing contamination from surfaces so far as reasonably practicable, cleaning/sanitising and equipment design in regulations 13(f), 14 and 19. These are food-business requirements in their applicable context, not a universal retail-cutlery approval certificate. For a proposed alloy, coating and use, have the importer and laboratory confirm the applicable evidence and test conditions before making safety claims. [FSANZ scope](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/food-standards-code/legislation) · [New Zealand Food Regulations original provisions](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/regulation/public/2015/0310/latest/DLM6684371.html)

## 7. Turn the evidence into a controlled purchase brief

A supermarket brief should specify the core 16/24-piece comparison, a separate gift-set trial, verified contents, the physical shelf pack and outer-carton dimensions, and the restocking review criteria. An e-commerce brief should add compatible replacement units, destination-specific parcel costs, exact photographs, care wording and an economical remedy for missing pieces. These are proposed tests derived from the observations, not claims that either assortment is already proven to sell.

Before artwork approval, maintain one signed specification for each bill of materials and two destination checks for Australia and New Zealand. Record which facts are supplied by the manufacturer, which are supported by a laboratory, and which remain unverified. Price and availability observations can change; refresh the relevant offers before a purchase decision. Public trade data, retailer material descriptions and an AI image cannot replace product testing or a negotiated supply agreement.
