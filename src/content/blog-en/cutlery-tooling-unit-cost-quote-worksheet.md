Two cutlery quotations can show different unit prices while charging differently for tooling, samples or branded packing. Comparing the unit-price line alone may choose the wrong option for the planned order. First make the physical specification and quoted delivery scope comparable; then use a simple **programme-cost worksheet**. The numbers below are hypothetical arithmetic, not Yumingxing prices or an offer.

Yumingxing's [buying information](/en/) distinguishes catalogue, private-label and new-tooling enquiries, with new molds or structural changes requiring feasibility review. Its [RFQ guide](/en/blog/what-to-include-in-a-cutlery-rfq/) asks for the model, quantities, components, packaging and destination. If these inputs differ, no formula makes the quotes equivalent. The [same-specification quote guide](/en/blog/compare-cutlery-quotes-same-specification/) addresses the wider comparison; this worksheet isolates **one-time versus quantity-based charges** after the specification is aligned.

## Ask each supplier to identify the charge basis

| Line | Record for each quotation |
|---|---|
| Sellable unit | One piece or one set; list exactly what it contains. |
| Quantity and unit price | Ordered units, currency, price per sellable unit and validity period. |
| One-time project charges | Tooling, sample or setup charge, only if actually quoted; record what is included and when it is payable. |
| Other separately priced items | Branding, retail packing, inspection or freight where excluded from unit price; keep delivery terms consistent. |
| Repeat-order treatment | Whether a tooling charge recurs, any ownership or storage terms, and what happens after a design revision; request written confirmation. |

For an agreed order quantity **Q**, calculate a comparison subtotal as **Q × quoted unit price + separately quoted one-time charges + other separately quoted charges**. Do not add an item twice if the unit price already includes it. Do not insert zero for an unknown charge: mark it **not quoted** and ask for clarification. Compare only quotations in the same currency, unit definition, specification, packaging scope and delivery basis; otherwise first normalize those terms or keep the offers in separate columns.

## Example: an arithmetic check, not a price benchmark

Suppose two *fictional* offers cover 1,000 identical sellable sets on the same quoted basis. Offer A is 4.00 currency units per set with no separate setup charge: **1,000 × 4.00 = 4,000**. Offer B is 3.70 per set plus a separately quoted 500 setup charge: **1,000 × 3.70 + 500 = 4,200**. At that quantity, A has the lower comparison subtotal even though B has the lower unit price. This calculation excludes taxes, logistics, inspection and any other item not included in both fictional offers; it is not a landed-cost calculation.

For a possible later order, do not automatically spread the first order's charge across future units. Ask in writing whether tooling is reusable, who controls it, and whether modifications trigger a new charge.
