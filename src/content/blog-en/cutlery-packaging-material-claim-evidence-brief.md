Replacing a cutlery sleeve or insert with a different material is a design decision. Printing “recyclable” or “eco-friendly” on that pack is a separate claim that needs evidence for the **actual finished package and sales market**. Do not let an attractive mock-up settle either question.

The [US EPA's sustainable-packaging overview](https://www.epa.gov/smm/sustainable-packaging) discusses material use and recovery as different parts of packaging decisions. The [US FTC Green Guides summary](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/environmental-claims-summary-green-guides) explains that environmental claims, including recyclability, require appropriate qualification and support in the US. Rules differ by destination; these US sources are not a substitute for local market review. No claim is made here about a Yumingxing package.

## Compare the real pack, one layer at a time

| Field | Buyer requests | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pack bill of materials | Sleeve, insert, window, coating, adhesive and label material for the approved version | Identify every layer rather than calling the whole pack “paper” |
| Protection | Sample trial for scratches, edge contact, movement and opening experience | Keep the cutlery protected through the agreed route |
| Dimensions and quantity | Packed size, pieces per retail unit, units per carton and revised weight | Recheck shelf, carton and shipping assumptions |
| Environmental wording | Exact proposed phrase, target country and evidence for the completed pack | Approve, qualify or remove the claim after market review |
| Change control | Artwork and material revision, approver and effective order | Prevent an old claim from following a changed material |

Ask the pack supplier for specifications and supporting documents for each material, then test the assembled pack with the intended stainless-steel pieces. A material being technically recyclable somewhere does not establish collection access where the customer buys it. Likewise, less material is not automatically the best answer if product damage increases. Record both the protection result and the claim review before printing.

The purchase order should name the approved pack revision and the exact wording permitted for each destination. Keep unverified language off artwork until the buyer's local compliance reviewer has checked the relevant rules and evidence.
