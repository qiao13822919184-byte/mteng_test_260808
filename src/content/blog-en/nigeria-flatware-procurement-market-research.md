*Market data checked: 19 November 2026. SONCAP workflow and provider-status update checked: 25 November 2026. Public-source research for supermarket procurement teams and e-commerce sales companies; no store visits, purchases or product testing.*

*Cover: AI editorial illustration of generic stainless steel cutlery and packaging, not a factory photograph, tested product or identified retail SKU.*

Nigeria supports a more specific buying question than “Is the market growing?”: **can a buyer distinguish replenishment utensils, portable pairs and family sets clearly enough to price and fulfil them profitably?** Our dated observations span two physical retail chains and two online sales channels. They show available product formats, but also material gaps and a catalogue/image mismatch. Treat that information as a starting specification, not proof of national demand.

## 1. Imports: a volatile value series, not stainless steel consumption

We queried Nigeria as reporter, imports from the world and China, for 2023–2025 in UN Comtrade’s H6/HS2022 series. The values below are current US dollars on the recorded CIF basis, rounded to millions; China shares use unrounded values. [2023 data](https://comtradeapi.un.org/public/v1/preview/C/A/HS?period=2023&reporterCode=566&cmdCode=8215,821191&flowCode=M&partnerCode=0,156&maxRecords=500), [2024 data](https://comtradeapi.un.org/public/v1/preview/C/A/HS?period=2024&reporterCode=566&cmdCode=8215,821191&flowCode=M&partnerCode=0,156&maxRecords=500), [2025 data](https://comtradeapi.un.org/public/v1/preview/C/A/HS?period=2025&reporterCode=566&cmdCode=8215,821191&flowCode=M&partnerCode=0,156&maxRecords=500).

| Year | HS8215 world / China, USD m | China share | HS821191 world / China, USD m | China share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 1.172 / 0.957 | 81.6% | 0.116 / 0.097 | 83.7% |
| 2024 | 2.642 / 2.156 | 81.6% | 0.191 / 0.189 | 98.8% |
| 2025 | 1.423 / 1.389 | 97.6% | 0.173 / 0.166 | 96.2% |

HS8215 world import value fell 46.1% in 2025 against 2024, while China supplied 97.6% of the 2025 value. Fixed-blade table knives under HS821191 declined 9.6%; China’s share was 96.2%. These are different scopes, not contradictory estimates of one market. HS8215 includes several table/kitchen articles and materials; it does not isolate stainless steel. Table knives also require a separate heading. Do not add the columns and call the result the Nigerian stainless steel flatware market.

Most returned records are Comtrade aggregates, rather than individually reported heading-level entries. The 2024 world net weight for HS8215 is missing; the 2025 weight carries an estimation flag. A null is not zero, and weight is not a number of retail sets. Exchange rates, timing, product mix and reporting can change import value without equivalent changes in consumer use. The practical implication is to test replenishment against local sell-through before converting a single strong import year into a large order.

## 2. Retail and e-commerce: separate the denominators

The National Bureau of Statistics reports real Trade-sector growth of **2.40% year on year in Q2 2026**, with a **17.93%** contribution to real GDP. This is wholesale/retail value added, not retail turnover or flatware sales. A national retail-only sales total was not established in this research. [NBS GDP release and original download](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/147).

The NITDA/NBS *Nigeria Tech Outlook 2026* survey found **6.6% of adults** had bought anything online in the preceding **three months**. Its telephone sample comprised **2,147 adults**, with fieldwork from **26 September to 9 October 2026**. Telephone coverage and respondent-selection limitations matter; this is neither an annual penetration rate nor a cutlery purchase rate. The report itself identifies a gap in official e-commerce series matching its definition. [Original report, methodology and chapter 5](https://nitda.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/NITDA26-NG-TECH-OUTLOOK-compressed.pdf).

There is also fresh platform evidence: Jumia’s **18 November 2026** Q3 release reports **30% year-on-year Nigerian GMV growth** in its physical-goods business highlights. That is one platform across categories, not growth in all Nigerian e-commerce or flatware. [Jumia original results, page 2](https://s205.q4cdn.com/370993272/files/content_files/OS-Jumia-EX-99-1-Q3-2026.pdf). Our inference: an online pilot is justified, but its scale must be earned through delivered orders and contribution, not a national market-size claim.

NG01–NG06 explicitly name stainless steel, but none establishes a steel grade, thickness or finish chemistry. Their single-utensil titles do not establish the sales-pack count. NG07–NG09 describe portable pairs; their material is unconfirmed and they are excluded from stainless price comparisons. Character branding is an observed retail feature, not permission to reproduce licensed artwork.

NG10 is particularly important: the catalogue says Everwinhome, while the linked packaging image says MAC-DON, stainless steel and 24 pieces. **The product-to-image identity is unresolved**, so neither its depicted count nor material can be treated as confirmed for the offered SKU. NG11’s description and image support six teaspoons; the image claims stainless steel, without a grade. NG12’s “silver” wording is not a material specification.

Packaging evidence is equally uneven. NG07/NG08 images show a storage case; NG11 shows a hanging retail sleeve/card. These are image observations, not verified export packaging. Retail-pack dimensions, shipping weights and master-carton quantities remain unknown for every row. We excluded an apparently priced Supermart Rossetti 1491 listing because its public product record marked it unavailable, and did not replace blocked marketplace pages with old search prices. The sample proves assortment visibility, not sales velocity.

## 4. Supermarkets: use format clarity to design the first shelf test

The NGN3,300 MINISO listings establish a visible price point for design-led utensil offers in those stores. They do not establish a national entry price, or the price of a confirmed multi-piece pack. The NGN5,699.99 six-teaspoon listing provides a different format reference. Dividing that price by six gives about **NGN950.00 per teaspoon** before delivery; this is a pack arithmetic example, not proof that its steel, weight or finish matches MINISO.

For a supermarket buyer, propose separate trials for replenishment and complete settings. Require the supplier to enumerate each utensil, state the retail-unit quantity and confirm whether a rack, case or serving piece is included in the advertised total. Do not build a family-set price ladder from the conflicted NG10 listing. Obtain a correctly matched sample, packaging proof and bill of materials first.

Keep the initial assortment narrow enough to measure availability and weekly unit sales by store. Record markdowns, missing-piece complaints and replenishment lead time separately. Reorder points should use the buyer’s observed weekly demand multiplied by replenishment lead time, plus an explicitly chosen buffer. Neither this sample nor the trade series supplies those inputs.

## 5. E-commerce: sell a defined basket and measure delivered contribution

Portable pairs and replenishment utensils offer different baskets. A low product price can be overwhelmed by fulfilment costs, while a larger set can create more counting and damage exposure. Request a postcode-specific freight quote and a measured packed sample before comparing the two. Do not assume one Lagos storefront delivers nationwide.

| Pilot decision | Supermarket buyer | E-commerce sales company |
|---|---|---|
| Offer architecture | Separate replenishment units from complete settings; verify pack count | Separate portable pairs, replenishment packs and full sets in listing variants |
| Packaging evidence | Shelf-facing quantity, barcode, hanging or shelf stability | Packed dimensions/weight, separated utensils, transit and opening checks |
| Release condition | Matching physical sample, artwork and bill of materials | Matching photos, material/quantity text, delivery scope and support process |
| Scale condition | Store-level sell-through and replenishment economics | Delivered-order contribution after fulfilment, payment/platform fees and returns |

Use a contribution worksheet: customer receipts less product cost, import/inland costs, platform/payment charges, packing, delivery subsidy and returns/loss allowance. Recoverable tax should be distinguished from permanent cost. Obtain actual fee schedules and assess the importer’s tax position; no national margin or rejection-rate assumption is supplied here. For paired utensils, show the contents outside any case, identify the case separately and avoid implying an unverified material grade or coating.

## 6. Import, food-contact and labelling checks before committing stock

**Import route.** Nigeria’s official trade portal describes importer registration, Form M and the customs documentation/PAAR process. Its downloadable tariff schedule distinguishes fixed-blade table knives **8211910000**, other non-precious-plated assorted sets **8215200000**, and other non-set articles **8215990000**. The downloaded rows show reference duty/VAT fields of **20% / 7.5%**; they are not a binding quotation. The government has announced **2026 tariff amendments**, whose complete SKU-level effect we could not reconcile with this undated download. Have the importer confirm the current code, origin, applicable amendments, levies and assessment bases. Do not simply add all spreadsheet percentage columns. [Import procedure](https://tip.nsw.gov.ng/trading-with-nigeria/import-procedure), [tariff download](https://tip.nsw.gov.ng/api/tariffs/download), [official amendment notice](https://fmino.gov.ng/nigeria-customs-service-implements-2026-fiscal-policy-measures-and-tariff-amendments/).

**Conformity and food-contact scope.** SON describes SONCAP as pre-shipment conformity verification with Product Certification and consignment certification stages. Ask the importer and SON-approved assessment body to establish the route before shipment; the food exemption is not evidence that reusable utensils are exempt. [SON programme](https://son.gov.ng/soncapservice/). SON’s own standards catalogue lists **NIS ISO8442-2:2018** for stainless steel and silver-plated cutlery. The catalogue identifies a relevant standard, but does not itself prove a particular SKU’s mandatory edition or test plan. Obtain the applicable full text and written scope confirmation; the full standard was not acquired here. [SON catalogue](http://library.son.gov.ng/?g=mech&s=6&t=groups-opac).

**Certification workflow update.** SON's migration notice, published on 31 March 2026, sets **27 March 2026** as the transition date. Applications for Import Permits (Product and Shipment Certificates) and SONCAP Defaults move to the National Single Window (NSW). For offshore inspection through Internationally Accredited Firms, Product and Shipment Certificate generation continues on the SON portal; **activation for Form M, PAAR and cargo clearance takes place on NSW**. The importer should identify both the correct issuing route and activation step before placing an order; these are different operations. [SON migration notice](https://son.gov.ng/2026/03/31/mandatory-migration-of-soncap-and-import-permit-processes-to-the-national-single-window-nsw-platform/).

Bureau Veritas's **October 2024** SONCAP guide states that import packaging labels should include English. This is historical assessment-body guidance, not a universal statutory label template or evidence of current provider approval. SON published a notice of Bureau Veritas's voluntary withdrawal from SONCAP on **5 August 2026**. The publication date alone does not establish the transition terms for a particular certificate. Select an assessment body against SON's current authorisation and confirm the applicable labelling requirements with it. [Historical guide](https://cdn2-verigates.bureauveritas.com/sites/verigates/files/2024-10/NIGERIA%20%28SONCAP%29%20Rev%208.4.pdf), [SON withdrawal notice](https://son.gov.ng/2026/08/05/bureau-veritas-vuluntary-withdrawal-from-soncap-scheme/). Agree material declarations, coating/handle details, intended contact conditions and applicable tests with the buyer and assessment body. A colour description or overseas certificate does not settle Nigerian acceptance, and no sampled item has been laboratory-verified by us.

**Consumer information.** The FCCPA requires understandable information, price disclosure and non-misleading trade descriptions under sections **114–116**, and addresses safe/quality goods under **131–132**. Translate that into consistent shelf, pack and online quantity/material descriptions and a workable complaints process; do not equate consumer remedies with an unrestricted commercial warranty. [FCCPA original](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf). The unresolved catalogue/image mapping is therefore a purchasing issue to correct before listing, not merely a photography detail.

## 7. The procurement brief to send next

Request an itemised proposal for one replenishment format and one complete-setting format, with a separate optional portable pair. Specify the target sales channel, intended retail price range, store locations or delivery areas, and the precise count of knives, forks and spoons. Ask for steel and surface declarations, unit dimensions/weight, retail-pack and carton measurements, matched artwork, sample terms, lead time and replenishment terms. Leave order quantity and factory price open until those facts are available.

The evidence supports testing distinct formats and improving specification accuracy. It does not establish a nationally preferred colour, a guaranteed price premium or a ready-made landed margin.
